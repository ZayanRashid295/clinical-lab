# Nervous System — Organ Systems
## Topic: Cerebrovascular disease
## Clinical Case
A 72-year-old man with a history of atrial fibrillation (not on anticoagulation) and hypertension presents to the emergency department 2 hours after the sudden onset of left-sided weakness and facial droop. His wife noticed he was unable to speak clearly when she found him.

**Physical Examination:**
- **Blood pressure:** 160/95 mm Hg
- **Heart rate:** 110/min, irregularly irregular
- **Neurological examination:**
  - Left facial droop (upper and lower face)
  - Left arm weakness (2/5 strength)
  - Left leg weakness (3/5 strength)
  - Expressive aphasia (cannot form words)
  - NIH Stroke Scale: 12

Non-contrast CT head shows no acute hemorrhage. The patient's last known normal time was 1.5 hours ago.

---

## Question
What is the most appropriate immediate treatment?

**A.** Aspirin 325 mg

### Choice A Explanation — Aspirin
**Why it's wrong:** While aspirin is important for secondary prevention, it is not the immediate treatment for acute ischemic stroke. Aspirin should be started 24 hours after tPA (if given) or immediately if tPA is not indicated.

**B.** Alteplase (tPA) for thrombolysis ✅

### Choice B Explanation — Alteplase (tPA)
**Why it's correct:**
- **Time window:** Patient is within 4.5 hours of symptom onset (1.5 hours ago)
- **No contraindications:** No hemorrhage on CT, no recent surgery, BP < 185/110
- **Evidence-based:** tPA improves functional outcomes when given within the time window
- **Standard of care:** First-line treatment for acute ischemic stroke meeting criteria
- **Mechanism:** Dissolves the clot causing the stroke

**Contraindications to consider:**
- Intracranial hemorrhage (not present)
- Recent major surgery (not mentioned)
- Severe hypertension (BP 160/95 is acceptable)
- Coagulopathy (not mentioned)

**C.** Mechanical thrombectomy

### Choice C Explanation — Mechanical thrombectomy
**Why it's wrong:** While mechanical thrombectomy is an important treatment, it is typically used for large vessel occlusions (proximal MCA, ICA) and is often combined with tPA. For this patient, tPA should be given first, and thrombectomy may be considered if there's a large vessel occlusion.

**D.** Anticoagulation with heparin

### Choice D Explanation — Anticoagulation with heparin
**Why it's wrong:** Anticoagulation is not the immediate treatment for acute stroke. It may be considered later for secondary prevention in atrial fibrillation, but giving heparin acutely can worsen outcomes and is contraindicated with tPA.

**E.** Blood pressure reduction

### Choice E Explanation — Blood pressure reduction
**Why it's wrong:** Aggressive BP reduction in acute stroke can worsen perfusion to ischemic brain tissue. BP should only be lowered if > 220/120 (for tPA candidates) or > 185/110 (if giving tPA). This patient's BP of 160/95 does not require immediate reduction.

**Correct Answer:** B

---

## Explanation

### Overview
This patient presents with an **acute ischemic stroke** in the right middle cerebral artery (MCA) territory, evidenced by left-sided hemiparesis and expressive aphasia. The history of atrial fibrillation suggests a cardioembolic source. The patient is within the **thrombolytic window** (< 4.5 hours from symptom onset), making **alteplase (tPA)** the appropriate immediate treatment.

---

### Clinical Presentation
- **Sudden onset:** Classic for stroke
- **Left hemiparesis:** Right MCA territory (affects left side of body)
- **Expressive aphasia:** Broca's area involvement (left frontal lobe)
- **Atrial fibrillation:** Major risk factor for cardioembolic stroke
- **No hemorrhage on CT:** Required before giving tPA
- **Within time window:** 1.5 hours from onset (< 4.5 hour limit)

---

### Stroke Localization

| Finding | Localization | Significance |
|---------|--------------|-------------|
| Left hemiparesis | Right MCA | Contralateral weakness |
| Expressive aphasia | Left frontal (Broca's) | Language production center |
| Facial droop (upper + lower) | Central facial nerve | Distinguishes from Bell's palsy |

## Management Approach
1. **Immediate (within 4.5 hours):**
   - **Alteplase (tPA)** 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg)
   - 10% as bolus, 90% over 1 hour
   - Monitor for complications (bleeding, angioedema)

2. **Concurrent:**
   - Continuous neurological monitoring
   - Blood pressure management (keep < 185/110 during tPA)
   - NPO until swallow evaluation

3. **Post-tPA (24 hours):**
   - Start aspirin 81-325 mg daily
   - Consider anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation (after 24-48 hours)

4. **Secondary prevention:**
   - Anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation (warfarin or DOAC)
   - Blood pressure control
   - Statin therapy
   - Lifestyle modifications
