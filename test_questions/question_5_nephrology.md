# Renal, Urinary Systems & Electrolytes — Organ Systems
## Topic: Acute kidney injury
## Clinical Case
A 68-year-old man with diabetes and hypertension presents with 3 days of decreased urine output and swelling in his legs. He was started on lisinopril 2 weeks ago for blood pressure control. He takes metformin for diabetes.

**Laboratory Studies:**
- **Serum creatinine:** 3.2 mg/dL (baseline 1.0 mg/dL)
- **BUN:** 45 mg/dL
- **Urine output:** 200 mL over 24 hours
- **Urinalysis:** 
  - Specific gravity: 1.010
  - Protein: 2+ (300 mg/dL)
  - No casts, no cells
- **Serum potassium:** 5.8 mEq/L (elevated)
- **Serum sodium:** 138 mEq/L

Physical examination reveals 2+ pitting edema in both lower extremities. Blood pressure is 140/90 mm Hg.

---

## Question
What is the most likely cause of this patient's acute kidney injury?

**A.** Diabetic nephropathy  
**B.** Acute tubular necrosis (ATN)  
**C.** Glomerulonephritis  
**D.** Urinary tract obstruction  
**E.** ACE inhibitor-induced renal failure (prerenal azotemia) ✅

**Correct Answer:** E

---

## Explanation

### Overview
This patient has **acute kidney injury (AKI)** likely caused by **ACE inhibitor-induced prerenal azotemia**. The recent initiation of lisinopril (2 weeks ago), combined with the clinical presentation (decreased urine output, elevated creatinine, bland urinalysis), strongly suggests that the ACE inhibitor has caused renal hypoperfusion, particularly in the setting of bilateral renal artery stenosis or volume depletion.

---

### Clinical Presentation
- **Recent medication change:** Lisinopril started 2 weeks ago (temporal relationship)
- **Decreased urine output:** Oliguria (< 400 mL/day)
- **Rapid creatinine rise:** From 1.0 to 3.2 mg/dL (3.2x increase)
- **Bland urinalysis:** No casts or cells (suggests prerenal, not intrinsic renal disease)
- **Hyperkalemia:** Common with ACE inhibitors
- **Edema:** May be from fluid retention or heart failure

---

### AKI Classification

| Type | Urinalysis | FeNa | Common Causes |
|------|------------|------|---------------|
| Prerenal | Bland | < 1% | Volume depletion, ACE inhibitors, heart failure |
| Intrinsic | Casts/cells | > 2% | ATN, glomerulonephritis, interstitial nephritis |
| Postrenal | Normal or hematuria | Variable | Obstruction |

---

## Choice-by-Choice Explanations

### Choice A Explanation — Diabetic nephropathy
**Why it's wrong:** Diabetic nephropathy is a chronic, progressive condition that develops over years, not days. The rapid onset (2 weeks) and temporal relationship with lisinopril initiation point to an acute cause.

---

### Choice B Explanation — Acute tubular necrosis
**Why it's wrong:** ATN typically shows granular casts and muddy brown casts on urinalysis. This patient has a bland urinalysis, which is more consistent with prerenal azotemia.

---

### Choice C Explanation — Glomerulonephritis
**Why it's wrong:** Glomerulonephritis would show hematuria, proteinuria with casts (especially red cell casts), and often hypertension. While this patient has proteinuria, the absence of casts and cells, plus the temporal relationship with ACE inhibitor, points away from GN.

---

### Choice D Explanation — Urinary tract obstruction
**Why it's wrong:** Obstruction would typically cause anuria or fluctuating urine output, and might show hydronephrosis on imaging. The urinalysis and clinical picture don't strongly suggest obstruction.

---

### Choice E Explanation — ACE inhibitor-induced renal failure
**Why it's correct:**
- **Temporal relationship:** Symptoms started after lisinopril initiation (2 weeks ago)
- **Mechanism:** ACE inhibitors can cause renal hypoperfusion by:
  - Reducing efferent arteriolar tone (more than afferent)
  - Leading to decreased glomerular filtration pressure
  - Particularly problematic in bilateral renal artery stenosis, volume depletion, or heart failure
- **Bland urinalysis:** Consistent with prerenal azotemia (no intrinsic renal damage)
- **Hyperkalemia:** Common side effect of ACE inhibitors
- **Reversible:** Discontinuing the ACE inhibitor typically leads to recovery

---

## Management Approach
1. **Immediate:**
   - **Discontinue lisinopril** (and metformin if creatinine > 1.5)
   - Monitor urine output and creatinine
   - Address hyperkalemia if severe (> 6.0 or ECG changes)
   - Consider volume expansion if volume depleted

2. **Evaluation:**
   - Renal ultrasound to assess for renal artery stenosis
   - Assess volume status
   - Review other medications

3. **Alternative antihypertensive:**
   - Use non-ACE inhibitor/ARB medication
   - Consider calcium channel blocker or beta-blocker

4. **Follow-up:**
   - Monitor creatinine recovery (should improve within days to weeks)
   - Reassess need for ACE inhibitor after recovery
