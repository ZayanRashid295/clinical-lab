# Pathology — Endocrine  
## Clinical Case
A 13-year-old girl is brought to the clinic by her mother for a yearly physical examination. The patient feels well but is worried that she has not yet started puberty.  
**Temperature:** 36.7°C (98°F)  
**Blood pressure:** 152/91 mm Hg  
**Pulse:** 75/min  
**Respirations:** 18/min

Physical examination is significant for a lack of secondary sexual characteristics; a **blind-ending vagina** is noted on pelvic examination. Laboratory studies reveal **hypokalemia** and **low testosterone and estradiol levels**. Cytogenetic analysis shows a **46,XY karyotype**.

---

## Question
This patient most likely has deficiency of which of the following enzymes?

**A.** 5 alpha-reductase (11%)  
**B.** 17 alpha-hydroxylase (66%) ✅  
**C.** 11 beta-hydroxylase (8%)  
**D.** 17,20-lyase (7%)  
**E.** 3 beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (8%)

**Correct Answer:** B

---

## Explanation

### Overview
This patient is genetically male (46,XY) with features suggestive of **17α-hydroxylase deficiency**, a rare cause of congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH). This enzyme deficiency impairs both cortisol and androgen synthesis, leading to accumulation of precursor hormones and shunting toward the mineralocorticoid pathway.

---

### Clinical Presentation
- Hypertension from excess mineralocorticoid (11-deoxycorticosterone)  
- Hypokalemia from aldosterone-like effects  
- Sexual underdevelopment from androgen deficiency  
- Female external genitalia with XY karyotype (46,XY) results from lack of androgen action during fetal development. The blind-ending vagina occurs because anti-müllerian hormone (AMH) from fetal testes suppresses müllerian duct development, but without androgenic action, external female genitalia form.

## Enzyme Function (detailed)
The enzyme **17 alpha-hydroxylase (CYP17A1)** catalyzes:
1. **17-hydroxylation:** Pregnenolone → 17-hydroxypregnenolone and Progesterone → 17-hydroxyprogesterone  
2. **17,20-lyase activity:** 17-hydroxypregnenolone → DHEA and 17-hydroxyprogesterone → Androstenediol

This enzyme is expressed in both the adrenal glands (for cortisol synthesis) and gonads (for androgen synthesis). Without functional 17α-hydroxylase, both cortisol and androgen synthesis are impaired.

## Choice-by-Choice Explanations

### Choice A Explanation — 5 alpha-reductase
**Why it's wrong:** 5α-reductase deficiency leads to decreased DHT with normal testosterone. Patients can have ambiguous or female-appearing external genitalia at birth but often virilize at puberty due to increased testosterone. **Hypertension and hypokalemia are not features.**

---

### Choice B Explanation — 17 alpha-hydroxylase
**Why it's correct:**  
- Explains the combination of **hypertension** + **hypokalemia** (mineralocorticoid excess) and **46,XY with female phenotype** (androgen deficiency).  
- **Low testosterone and estradiol** are consistent with impaired androgen synthesis.  
- Management focuses on glucocorticoid replacement and controlling mineralocorticoid effects.

---

### Choice C Explanation — 11 beta-hydroxylase
**Why it's wrong:** 11β-hydroxylase deficiency causes accumulation of 11-deoxycortisol and 11-deoxycorticosterone (a mineralocorticoid); however, it is typically associated with **virilization** because androgen production is increased — not sexual underdevelopment.

---

### Choice D Explanation — 17,20-lyase
**Why it's wrong:** 17,20-lyase deficiency causes decreased androgen production but **normal cortisol** and therefore **no hypertension**; the case here has clear mineralocorticoid excess.

---

### Choice E Explanation — 3 beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase
**Why it's wrong:** 3β-HSD defects impair multiple steroid pathways and have a different biochemical and clinical signature; the presentation differs from the classic features seen in this case.
